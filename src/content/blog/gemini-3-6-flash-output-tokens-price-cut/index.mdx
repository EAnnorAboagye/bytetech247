## Quick Answer

Gemini 3.6 Flash reportedly uses 17% fewer output tokens than Gemini 3.5 Flash for comparable tasks, and launched at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, with a 1M-token context window. That's two cost levers moving at once: a lower per-token rate, and fewer tokens spent per task.

## What's actually changing

Tech-press launch coverage (9to5google.com, "Google launches Gemini 3.6 Flash and 3.5 Flash-Lite," dated 2026-07-21) reports that Gemini 3.6 Flash uses 17% fewer output tokens than Gemini 3.5 Flash for comparable tasks. That figure is sourced to reputable tech-press coverage, not Google's own blog post directly, and is stated honestly as such.

Separately, and more concretely confirmed by the same launch coverage, Gemini 3.6 Flash launched at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, prices confirmed on [Google's own Gemini API pricing page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing), with a 1M-token context window.

## Why two levers matter more than one

A comparison that only checks the per-token rate misses half the picture here. If Gemini 3.6 Flash really does produce fewer output tokens for the same task, the cost saving compounds: a lower rate applied to a smaller token count, not just a lower rate applied to the same count as before. Two providers can advertise similar-looking per-token prices and still produce meaningfully different bills for the same workload, once actual token counts per task are accounted for.

That is exactly the trap a sticker-price-only comparison falls into, and exactly why [Same Prompt, Different Bill](/ai-productivity/same-prompt-different-bill-gpt-claude-gemini/) treats token count and price per token as two separate numbers to check, not one.
